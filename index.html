Generative AI

What is it? How is it harmful? What can we do to stop it?

What is generative AI?

Generative AI is artificial intelligence trained to create images, video, audio or software code once given a prompt. Examples of generative AI models include ChatGPT, Claude, and Google Gemini. From its creation in the 1960s, generative AI has significantly spiked in popularity following the release of ChatGPT in 2022, this is also known as the “AI boom”. Since then, it has been integrated into almost every company; today, 78% of global companies are using AI in their daily operations. Generative AI seems like the future of technology, but its has dangerous effects and implications for the future.

How is generative AI different to other AI?

As its name suggests, Generative AI generates entirely new items based on prior training. While other AI is given instructions once and does that specific job; recognising algorithms, images, videos, and more. Examples of this are facial recognition, and the algorithms in social media and streaming services. To be more specific, Artificial intelligence is classified into seven categories, and it can get a bit complicated—given that four out of the seven are only theoretical and don't exist yet. The AI that we have now is able to perform tasks based on pre-set rules and data, to make decisions, perform tasks, and generate things. In the future, AI may gain an increased understand human emotions and social cues, and even develop its own self awareness, surpassing human intelligence. This is referred to as strong AI or super intelligence AI.

The problem

While generative AI can maximise efficiency, aid in research and development, and more, it poses important environmental and ethical issues. A very significant catalyst for environmental pollution caused by generative AI and deep learning models (AI trained to understand complex problems) is that they require data centers. Generative AI data centers are needed to house the massive computing power, memory, and cooling systems needed to train and run generative AI models. Extreme amounts of electricity and freshwater used to power and cool the generative AI causes strenuous impacts on the environment, and data centers produce noise pollution which irritates local biodiversity. Outside of the data centers, generative AI chat bots such as ChatGPT cause large amounts of cognitive decline.Ultimately, generative AI has many pros mostly involving boosting efficiency, but its significant cons should be taken into consideration by the AI companies producing, but also the users consuming.
